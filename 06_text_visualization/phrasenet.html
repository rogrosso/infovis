Phrase Net

Phrase Net, Ham et all. 2009, is an advanced text visualization technique that operates at the intersection of multiple tasks. It analyzes:
  • Connections among entities, including the network structure itself, as well as nodes and edges.
  • Semantic or structural relationships between words, with the connector pattern defining the type of relationship being visualized.
  • Collocation and co-occurrence, where the edge weight indicates how frequently two words appear together in a specific pattern.
Phrase Net highlights the semantic relationships that an author constructs through specific linguistic patterns. For instance, the connector "and" illustrates the concepts that the author coordinates and groups into the same category. The connector "of" reveals the elements that the author considers to be part of something else. The connector "is" shows how the author defines and identifies things through metaphor and description. The network structure adds another layer of meaning: highly connected nodes (large, prominent words) act as conceptual hubs around which other concepts orbit. Conversely, weakly connected or peripheral nodes represent more specific or localized details. In this example, we have implemented three different connectors:
  • "X and Y" displays the concepts that Whitman coordinates and associates. Given his famous catalogic and enumerative style (e.g., "of the grass, of the soil, of the sea..."), this approach will produce a rich, dense network, revealing his worldview and showing how different things belong together in his universe.
  • "X of Y" illustrates possession and composition relationships, indicating what belongs to what and what contains what.
  • "X is Y" signifies identity and metaphor.
